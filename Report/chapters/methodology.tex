\chapter{Methodology}
\label{chap:methodology}

This chapter specifies the complete chain from literature evidence to held-out
predictions. The unit recorded in the database is an experimental condition,
but the unit of scientific transfer is a publication: rows from one paper share
apparatus, materials, measurement practice, and often a common experimental
campaign. The study design therefore treats row-level interpolation and
paper-level extrapolation as distinct estimands, confines every data-adaptive
operation to training partitions, and preserves a machine-readable audit trail
from each reported score to its source rows, split assignment, candidate
configuration, and fitted model. These principles were fixed before comparing
candidate performance.

\section{Assembly of the microgravity flammability database}
\label{sec:database-assembly}

\subsection{Literature screening and inclusion}

The database was assembled before either machine-learning pipeline was designed.
Approximately 130 experimental papers and technical reports published from 1966
onward were screened for solid-fuel combustion under microgravity,
partial-gravity, or spacecraft-relevant atmospheric and flow conditions. Only
about 87 publications exposed sufficiently resolved observations to map ignition
or flame-spread rate (FSR) into individual database rows. A paper was useful for
modeling only when the experiment, specimen, environment, and measured outcome
could be connected at row level; a qualitative conclusion without recoverable
conditions could support the literature review but not supervised learning.

The resulting corpus is deliberately broader than one material family or rig.
It includes wires, flat specimens, cylinders, and spheres tested on the ground,
in drop towers, parabolic aircraft, and spacecraft. Each row retains a full
human-readable citation, authors, and DOI or report identifier where available.
That provenance is essential because observations from one publication are
clustered and because ambiguous values must be checked against the source rather
than treated as anonymous numbers.

The overview is not a balanced factorial design. A median of 32 rows per
paper coexists with campaigns that contribute several hundred nearby
conditions from a single rig. That source-size structure is why a random
row split is optimistic: conditions from a large paper can appear on both
sides of an interpolation split, whereas holding out that paper removes a
large, internally correlated block. The same card also shows why two
supervised tasks, not five, are feasible: ignition labels are essentially
complete, numeric FSR covers about half the table, and the remaining
flammability outputs are too sparse to train.

\subsection{Extraction strategy}

The first attempted extraction workflow was a conventional optical character
recognition (OCR) pipeline. It was not viable for this corpus. The sources range
from old scanned papers to long NASA memoranda; notation, units, table
structures, and page layouts differ; scans and plotted markers can be blurred;
and many results appear only in graphs rather than machine-readable text.
Generic OCR also confuses subscripts and symbols, separates values from their
headers, and cannot reliably infer which experimental condition belongs to a
plotted point. Applying it at scale would have created a large table with an
unacceptably hidden transcription error rate.

The adopted workflow was therefore AI-assisted but human-verified:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item the source was read to identify the experiment, sample, controlled
  conditions, reported outputs, and units;
  \item an AI extraction pass was used to recover as much structured information
  as possible for each experimental row;
  \item every extracted row was then checked against the paper by the database
  curator, and missing or incorrectly associated values were completed or
  corrected manually;
  \item units and categorical names were standardized while the original
  citation and explanatory notes were retained for traceability.
\end{enumerate}

AI was used as an extraction accelerator, not as an authority. This distinction
is important for scientific data: a fluent transcription can still attach a
correct number to the wrong series, silently flatten a logarithmic axis, or
invent precision that the graph does not contain. Manual review remained the
acceptance step. Values read from plots were stored only to the precision
supported by marker size and axis resolution; visually inferred trailing digits
were not treated as measured information. Ambiguous series assignments or units
were resolved from captions and surrounding text, or left missing when the
source did not support a defensible interpretation.

For dense plots, the points were digitized with WebPlotDigitizer
\cite{webplotdigitizer}. Axis calibration was set from the published ticks,
including explicit log-axis selection where required; each color or marker
series was traced separately; overlapping points were inspected at high zoom;
and the exported coordinates were checked against axis limits and values quoted
in the text. This procedure was preferred to AI-internal OCR because exact point
coordinates, logarithmic scaling, and overlapping markers were otherwise
unreliable.

\subsection{Row structure, outputs, and quality control}

The final schema groups fields into citation, specimen, flow, apparatus,
outputs, and notes. Inputs cover geometry, dimensions, fuel and core
thermophysical properties, oxygen concentration, diluent, gas properties,
pressure, flow, dimensionless transport groups, gravity, facility, and ignition
conditions. Flammability is documented through the five outputs introduced in
\cref{tab:flammability-variables}: ignition, flame length, FSR, heat-release
rate, and smoke/aerosols. Not every paper reports every output.

The raw table uses a category banner as its first row and field names as its
second. Values were standardized to SI units wherever the source supported a
defensible conversion: length in metres, pressure in pascals, velocity in
\si{\metre\per\second}, power in watts, time in seconds, density in
\si{\kilogram\per\metre\cubed}, conductivity in
\si{\watt\per\metre\per\kelvin}, heat capacity in
\si{\joule\per\kilogram\per\kelvin}, and FSR in
\si{\metre\per\second}. Values whose physical dimension did not match a target
field were not coerced merely to fill a cell.

Ignition labels, fuel thermophysical properties, oxygen, and diluent are
essentially complete. Geometry, gravity, pressure, and ignition method remain
above 98\%. Flow velocity drops to 79.5\%. Numeric FSR is present in only
51.5\% of the raw rows, which is why the regression cohort is smaller than
the ignition cohort. Flame length (4.3\%), heat-release rate (1.1\%), and
smoke/aerosols (1.8\%) are too sparse to support a third supervised task.
Ignition power (18.8\%) and ignition time (45.6\%) are retained as predictors
where reported, with fold-local imputation for the remainder. Apparatus
names, facility labels, free-text material names, and notes are excluded from
model inputs because they can fingerprint a laboratory without encoding
physics. The schema therefore justifies both the two-task scope and the
need for fold-local imputation: missingness is a property of what
laboratories reported, not a random mask.

Quality control continued in code. Encodings were detected, empty rows and
columns removed, target labels normalized, exact modeling duplicates removed,
and unparseable values recorded as missing rather than guessed. Physical-domain
checks rejected impossible concentrations, non-positive pressures, and invalid
target encodings; conversions were performed before duplicate detection so that
equivalent observations expressed in different units could not survive as
independent rows. Canonical paper identity preferred a normalized DOI and
otherwise used a normalized citation. This produces identifiers of the form
\texttt{doi::<normalized-doi>} or
\texttt{citation::<normalized-citation>}. Cosmetic citation differences
therefore cannot place one publication in two paper groups. Dataset shape,
column roles, target prevalence, paper counts, and a content fingerprint are
written to validation records and checked again when frozen splits are loaded.

The two tasks use different valid subsets. After feature/target
standardization and deduplication, ignition classification contains 4,487 rows
from 87 canonical papers (3,383 ignition and 1,104 no ignition). FSR regression
contains 2,531 rows with a numeric FSR from 66 papers. The FSR papers contribute
between 1 and 193 rows, with median 22.5. Dataset fingerprints are persisted with
the splits and evaluations so that a result cannot silently refer to a changed
database.

\subsection{Source-size and class imbalance}
\label{sec:method-imbalance}

The assembled literature table is not a balanced factorial design. Two
imbalances dominate every subsequent modeling choice.

The first is source-size imbalance. Publications contribute very different
numbers of nearby conditions from a single rig, material batch, or campaign.
\Cref{fig:method-overview} already shows that the median paper is modest
while a handful of campaigns dominate the row count; the Pareto curve in
\cref{fig:method-imbalance}(a)
makes the cumulative effect explicit. After sorting papers from largest to
smallest contribution, 36 of 87 papers account for more than 80\% of all rows.
The remaining long tail contributes the last 20\%. A loss that treats every row
as an independent draw therefore over-represents densely sampled campaigns and
under-represents sparse but physically distinct sources. Paper weighting,
paper-grouped evaluation, and paper-cluster bootstrap intervals are the
methodological response to this structure.

The second is class imbalance on the ignition label. In the raw assembled
table of 5,057 rows, 3,875 observations (76.6\%) are ignition and 1,182
(23.4\%) are no ignition (\cref{fig:method-imbalance}(b)). After
task-specific cleaning the modeling cohort remains majority-positive: 3,383
ignition versus 1,104 no ignition. A classifier that always predicts ignition
would therefore already achieve about three-quarters accuracy and a
precision--recall baseline equal to the positive prevalence. Accuracy is not a
defensible selection metric. The ignition pipeline instead compares models with
ROC--AUC, PR--AUC, and Brier score, and it reports thresholded sensitivity,
specificity, precision, F1, balanced accuracy, and Matthews correlation. Class
weights and paper weights are tested as candidate recipes rather than assumed
to correct the imbalance automatically.

\section{Methods common to both machine-learning pipelines}
\label{sec:common-pipeline}

The two pipelines share a scientific contract even though one classifies
ignition and the other regresses flame-spread rate. Both estimate a mapping
from measured physical inputs \(\mathbf{x}\) to an observed target \(y\): the
classifier estimates \(\widehat p=P(y_{\mathrm{ign}}=1\mid\mathbf{x})\); the
regressor estimates \(\widehat v_{\mathrm{FSR}}\). Both freeze splits before
any model is compared, nest hyperparameter search inside outer training
partitions, and report interpolation and extrapolation as different questions.
\Cref{tab:method-design-contract} states the shared design contract; later
subsections give task-specific implementations.

The repeated splits estimate performance for the empirical mixture of
conditions and papers represented by the curated corpus. They do not constitute
an external prospective validation set, and paper-grouped performance should
not be interpreted as guaranteed transfer beyond the measured feature envelope.
The subsections below explain why the model families were chosen, how nested
evaluation is enforced, and which preprocessing and weighting choices are
common to both tasks.

\subsection{Benchmarking motivation and model families}

The closest prior machine-learning study of flame spread is Rivera, Jose, and
colleagues, who compiled approximately 1,200 electrical-wire observations and
compared linear regression, elastic net, stochastic gradient descent, Bayesian
ridge, kernel ridge, decision trees, \(k\)-nearest neighbours, gradient
boosting, support-vector regression, and a multilayer perceptron under random
ten-fold cross-validation \cite{riveraWireML}. That paper is the explicit
benchmark for family choice in this report. Nonlinear models were competitive
there; linear and shrinkage families lagged. The present work therefore
retains the families that were scientifically informative in that study,
upgrades gradient boosting to XGBoost, and adds random forest as a bagging
control for FSR. Linear, elastic-net, SGD, Bayesian-ridge, and kernel-ridge
regressors are not re-run: Jose already showed that they are a weak hypothesis
for heterogeneous flame-spread tables, and repeating them would inflate the
portfolio without a new physical question.

XGBoost is the primary tabular learner because Shwartz-Ziv and Armon found
gradient-boosted trees to be a strong, readily tuned baseline on heterogeneous
tables, including settings where deep models did not dominate
\cite{shwartzZiv2022tabular,chenGuestrin2016}. That prior is a starting point,
not a result. Decision trees remain as a transparent nonlinear baseline from
Jose. \(k\)-NN tests whether nearby experimental conditions are locally
predictive. SVM/SVR tests linear versus radial kernels in the engineered
physics space. MLPs test whether a smooth universal approximator helps once
the same nested, leakage-controlled protocol is imposed. Random forest is the
one family Jose did not include; it isolates bagging from boosting on the FSR
task, where source shift and outliers are acute.

\subsection{Features, leakage exclusions, and preprocessing}

Paper identifiers, authors, DOI, facility names, free text, and post-outcome
quantities are never used as predictors. Flame length, HRR, and smoke/aerosols
are excluded from both feature sets. Material names, rig names, and internal
apparatus geometry are likewise excluded: they can identify a laboratory
without encoding a physical mechanism. The appendix records the deployed
manifests. Ignition uses a fixed list of 32 numeric and five categorical
physics features. FSR detects 29 numeric and four categorical fields from the
same schema. Both cover fuel and core properties, geometry, oxygen and
diluent, pressure, gas transport, flow, gravity, and ignition conditions where
available. The persisted FSR manifest also contains categorical
\texttt{Ignition}; because this conflicts with explanatory documentation, it is
disclosed as a schema limitation rather than hidden.

Numeric missing values are replaced by the training-fold median for models
that cannot handle missingness; XGBoost retains native numeric missingness.
Distance, margin, and neural models use training-fold standardization.
Categorical missingness becomes an explicit ``Unknown'' level, and one-hot
encoding ignores a category first seen in a test fold. No vocabulary, scaler,
imputer, weight, or threshold is fit on outer test rows.

\subsection{Nested evaluation}

For a reader new to machine learning, three data roles must remain distinct:
\begin{description}
  \item[Training data] fit model parameters, such as tree splits or neural
  weights.
  \item[Validation data] choose hyperparameters, such as tree depth,
  regularization, or neighbourhood size.
  \item[Test data] measure the selected procedure once on observations not used
  for either fitting or selection.
\end{description}

The project implements this separation with nested evaluation
(\cref{fig:method-nested}). For outer split \(s\), candidate \(c\) and
hyperparameter setting \(\lambda\), the inner loop selects
\[
  \lambda^*_{c,s}
  =\arg\min_{\lambda\in\Lambda_c}
    \frac{1}{K_{\mathrm{in}}}\sum_{k=1}^{K_{\mathrm{in}}}
    L\!\left(y^{(k)},\widehat f^{(-k)}_{c,\lambda}(\mathbf{x}^{(k)})\right),
\]
with the direction of \(L\) reversed for metrics that are maximized. The
selected configuration is refitted on the complete outer training partition
and evaluated once on the untouched outer test partition. Preprocessing is part
of \(\widehat f\), not a preliminary operation on the full table. Candidate
comparison is paired on identical outer assignments, reducing avoidable
split-to-split noise. Repeating the outer split quantifies sensitivity to data
partitioning; repeated-fold scores are correlated and are not treated as
independent experimental replications.

\subsection{Interpolation versus extrapolation}

Both pipelines answer two different questions, illustrated in
\cref{fig:method-interp-extrap}:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Interpolation} holds out rows while allowing other rows from
  the same paper to remain in training. It asks whether the model predicts new
  conditions inside campaigns it partly knows.
  \item \textbf{Extrapolation} holds out complete canonical papers. It asks
  whether the procedure transfers to a new publication, rig, or campaign and is
  the primary generalization test.
\end{itemize}

The distinction prevents paper-specific sampling and apparatus signatures from
being mistaken for broad physical transfer. Rivera et al.\ themselves describe
random cross-validation as robustness within a compiled table rather than
strict held-source transfer \cite{riveraWireML}. The present design makes that
caveat operational: all candidates within a task share the same frozen outer
assignments, and grouped protocols assert zero paper overlap.

  \caption{Schematic of the two evaluation questions. Interpolation mixes
  train and test rows inside the same papers. Extrapolation holds out entire
  papers, which is the relevant test for a new campaign or material family.}

\subsection{Paper weighting, uncertainty, and refitting}

Source-size imbalance is treated as a modeling hypothesis, not a background
correction that is assumed to work. For loss \(\ell\), paper \(p(i)\) associated
with row \(i\), and training-fold paper size \(n_p\), the weighted empirical
risk is
\[
  \widehat R_w(f)=\frac{\sum_{i\in\mathcal T}w_{p(i)}
  \ell\!\left(y_i,f(\mathbf{x}_i)\right)}
  {\sum_{i\in\mathcal T}w_{p(i)}}.
\]
Both pipelines test three strategies (\cref{fig:method-paper-weights}).
Unweighted training, \(w_p=1\), gives paper \(p\) total influence proportional
to \(n_p\). Square-root weighting, \(w_p=n_p^{-1/2}\), reduces that influence
to \(\sqrt{n_p}\). Inverse weighting, \(w_p=n_p^{-1}\), gives each paper
approximately equal total influence. Counts and normalized weights are always
computed from the current training partition. Families that cannot accept
sample weights are trained on a deterministic probability-proportional resample
of that partition so that a weighting flag is never silently ignored.

\begin{figure}[htbp]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=0.98\textwidth]{method_paper_weights.pdf}
  \caption{Paper-weighting strategies shared by ignition and FSR candidates.
  \(n_p\) is the number of training rows from paper \(p\). The preferred
  strategy is selected empirically from nested scores, not assumed a priori.}
  \label{fig:method-paper-weights}
\end{figure}

Outer-fold means and standard deviations summarize partition sensitivity.
Ninety-five-percent percentile intervals use 2,000 bootstrap replicates.
Repeated outer predictions are first averaged for each observation; the
bootstrap then resamples observations for interpolation and complete canonical
paper clusters for extrapolation. This avoids counting repeated predictions of
the same row as independent evidence and preserves the sampling unit of the
question being asked. Intervals quantify resampling uncertainty within the
assembled corpus; they do not include literature-selection, transcription, or
measurement uncertainty.

Only after selection is complete is the chosen configuration refitted on all
labeled rows to create an inference artifact. That full-data model is deployable,
but its training fit is not used as evidence of generalization. Champion claims
apply only to candidates present in the persisted selection evidence: the
merged FSR comparison contains 25 of the 34 configured candidates, and the
formal ignition selection record omits available MLP shards. This distinction
prevents an unevaluated or incomplete candidate from becoming a champion by
configuration alone.

\section{Ignition-classification pipeline}
\label{sec:classification-method}

\subsection{Task, probabilities, and metrics}

Ignition is a binary label, but the model returns a probability
\(\widehat p\in[0,1]\). A threshold \(\tau\) produces the decision
\[
  \widehat y(\tau)=
  \begin{cases}
  1,&\widehat p\geq\tau,\\
  0,&\widehat p<\tau.
  \end{cases}
\]
A lower threshold catches more ignitions and usually increases false alarms; a
higher threshold does the reverse. Because the assembled table is
majority-positive, accuracy is not a defensible selection metric. Comparison
therefore uses ranking and calibration scores that do not require a threshold:
ROC--AUC, PR--AUC, and the Brier score
\[
  \mathrm{BS}=n^{-1}\sum_{i=1}^{n}(\widehat p_i-y_i)^2.
\]
ROC--AUC measures pairwise ranking across classes; PR--AUC is interpreted
relative to the positive-class prevalence; and Brier score assesses squared
probability error. Four operating points are then frozen from inner out-of-fold
predictions---maximum Matthews correlation (MCC), maximum F1, maximum balanced
accuracy, and maximum Youden
\(J=\mathrm{sensitivity}+\mathrm{specificity}-1\)---and applied to the outer
test fold without re-optimizing on test labels. Thresholded reporting adds
sensitivity, specificity, precision, F1, balanced accuracy, and MCC. The
threshold objective is therefore part of the procedure being evaluated, not a
post-hoc choice made after observing the test set.

\subsection{Candidate design}

The ignition portfolio contains 26 candidates across five families
(\cref{fig:method-ignition-candidates}). The design is an ablation of
\emph{how} a family is trained, not a search over dozens of unrelated
architectures. Every family includes an unweighted baseline. XGBoost, as the
primary model, receives the full imbalance grid; the other families are
structured Jose-aligned baselines.

Two imbalance mechanisms are treated as orthogonal. Paper weighting corrects
sampling bias from large campaigns. Class weighting rescales the loss by label
frequency. Focal loss \cite{lin2017focal} down-weights easy, confident
examples dynamically and is therefore an alternative class-imbalance
mechanism, not a third knob to stack on class weights. Focal candidates use
\(\gamma=2\) and \texttt{class\_weight: false} to avoid double-correcting the
majority class. Paper weighting is retained with focal loss because it

\subsection{Pipeline architecture}

\Cref{fig:method-ignition-pipeline} is the executable workflow. After the
feature manifest is built, four split protocols are frozen once and reused by
every candidate: stratified interpolation cross-validation, a stratified 80/20
holdout (consistency check only), paper-grouped extrapolation, and
leave-one-paper-out (LOPO). For each candidate and outer fold the inner search
runs on training rows only, four thresholds are frozen from inner out-of-fold
probabilities, and the outer test fold is scored once. Selection reads
persisted tables and does not refit. Only the winning interpolation and
extrapolation configurations are then trained on all labeled rows for later
inference.

  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=0.98\textwidth]{method_ignition_pipeline.pdf}
  \caption{Ignition classification pipeline. Splits are frozen before model
  comparison. Nested search and threshold selection are confined to outer
  training partitions. LOPO is a mandatory robustness gate for the
  extrapolation champion, not a substitute for grouped nested CV.}

\subsection{Interpolation protocol}

Ignition interpolation uses five-fold stratified cross-validation repeated for
seeds 42--44, giving 15 outer folds. Stratification keeps the ignition/no-
ignition proportion similar in each fold while rows are assigned independently,
so papers may appear on both sides. Within each outer training partition,
stratified inner folds search the family space. A separate stratified 80/20
holdout for each seed is a quick consistency check, not the primary selection
protocol.

Interpolation selection requires valid probabilities, complete folds, passed
integrity checks, and ROC--AUC fold standard deviation no greater than 0.15.
Candidates within 0.01 of the best ROC--AUC are resolved by higher PR--AUC,
lower uncertainty, and a declared simplicity order (decision tree, \(k\)-NN,
SVM, XGBoost, MLP). The resulting champion is refitted on all
ignition-labeled rows for within-domain inference.

\subsection{Extrapolation and leave-one-paper-out}

Ignition extrapolation uses \texttt{StratifiedGroupKFold}: all rows from one
paper stay together while each fold retains both classes where feasible. Five
paper-grouped folds repeated across three seeds again produce 15 outer tests.
The inner search is also paper-disjoint, so a paper held out for inner
validation cannot appear in the corresponding inner training fold.

The primary metric remains ROC--AUC, but eligibility additionally requires a
successful LOPO analysis. Each of the 87 papers is held out once. A
single-class paper cannot define its own ROC--AUC, so LOPO is interpreted
through pooled predictions, calibration, and the subset of papers for which a
metric is defined. The selected extrapolation model and its thresholds are
refitted on the full labeled cohort only after this paper-disjoint comparison
is frozen.

\section{Flame-spread-rate regression pipeline}
\label{sec:regression-method}

\subsection{Task and metrics}

Regression predicts a continuous FSR in \si{\metre\per\second}. There is no
classification threshold: quality is measured directly in target units. For
outer-test residuals \(e_i=\widehat y_i-y_i\),
\[
  \mathrm{RMSE}=\sqrt{n^{-1}\sum_i e_i^2},\qquad
  \mathrm{MAE}=n^{-1}\sum_i|e_i|.
\]
RMSE penalizes larger errors more strongly and is the primary selection metric.
MAE reports typical absolute error, \(R^2\) compares residual variance with a
mean predictor, and mean bias error \(n^{-1}\sum_i e_i\) records systematic
over- or underprediction.
MAPE is retained only as a secondary diagnostic because exact and near-zero
FSR values make percentage errors unstable. Predictions are always inverted
back to original FSR units before scoring, including for signed-log models.

Jose's headline \(R^2>0.9\) values were obtained on a narrower wire table
under random ten-fold cross-validation \cite{riveraWireML}. The present FSR
pipeline is built so that an analogous interpolation number can be reported,
but the scientific claim of this project is the paper-grouped result. The two
must not be averaged or substituted for one another.

\subsection{Candidate design}

The FSR portfolio contains 34 candidates across five families
(\cref{fig:method-fsr-candidates}). Each candidate tests one hypothesis:
does paper weighting improve transfer; does a robust loss help with
experimental outliers; does compressing a skewed target help optimization;
is local similarity enough; is the physics feature space already linear?
Jose's linear families are omitted. Random forest is added as the bagging
counterpart to XGBoost so that any later preference for trees is not
confounded with boosting.

\subsection{Pipeline architecture}

\Cref{fig:method-fsr-pipeline} shows the regression workflow. After role
detection and leakage exclusions, interpolation and extrapolation holdouts are
frozen. Each candidate is evaluated as an independent shard: nested search on
the outer training partition, then two fits of the selected configuration.
The \texttt{rd} condition uses real training rows only. The \texttt{rd\_bt}
condition appends 1,000 training rows sampled with replacement; copies inherit
their source paper identity and never enter the test partition. Bootstrap
augmentation is therefore a reweighting of observed conditions, as in Jose's
most conservative augmentation finding, not a generator of new experiments
\cite{riveraWireML}. Shards are merged before selection so that champions are
chosen from tables, not from a second round of training.

\subsection{Interpolation protocol}

FSR interpolation uses ten repeated, approximately 80/20 row holdouts with
seeds 42--51. Target values are binned into six quantiles where feasible so
that each split represents the strongly skewed FSR distribution. Papers may
occur on both sides. Inside each outer training partition, shuffled \(K\)-fold
validation compares hyperparameters. The selected recipe is fitted on all
2,024 training rows and tested on the 507 untouched rows for that repeat.

The selection policy rejects incomplete or non-finite evaluations, RMSE
coefficient of variation above 0.45, and mean \(R^2<-0.25\). Candidates within
2\% of the lowest RMSE form a tie band resolved by higher \(R^2\), lower
uncertainty, preference for real data over bootstrap copies, simpler family
(random forest, \(k\)-NN, SVR, XGBoost, MLP), and fewer features where the
feature sets differ.

\subsection{Extrapolation protocol}

FSR extrapolation also uses ten repeated 80/20 holdouts, but the unit assigned
to train or test is the paper. For each repeat, 50 candidate
\texttt{GroupShuffleSplit} partitions are generated and the most
target-balanced paper-disjoint split is retained. Each test contains 14 papers
and each training partition 52; row counts vary with paper size. Balance
screening reduces avoidable target-range changes without relaxing paper
separation. Because the screening criterion uses only target-distribution
summaries to choose among pre-generated paper partitions, it is treated as part
of split construction and is frozen before any candidate is evaluated. The
result remains a transfer estimate for this balance-screened split policy,
not for an arbitrary future collection of papers.

Inner tuning uses \texttt{GroupKFold} by paper. Extrapolation tie-breaks add
the interpolation--extrapolation gap so that a recipe is not favoured solely
for excellent within-paper fit. Cluster bootstrap intervals resample papers
rather than thousands of correlated rows. The winning
\((\mathrm{candidate},\,\mathrm{augmentation})\) pair is refitted on all
2,531 FSR rows and stored separately from the outer-fold evidence.

\section{Reproducibility and audit trail}
\label{sec:method-reproducibility}

Reproducibility is enforced through persisted interfaces rather than relying on
narrative description alone. The cleaned task table is fingerprinted; outer and
inner assignments are generated once from declared seeds and stored with
canonical paper identifiers; candidate hypotheses and search spaces are
versioned configuration files; and each evaluation shard records software
settings, selected hyperparameters, fold metrics, and row-level outer
predictions. Aggregation and champion selection read these artifacts without
training a model. Integrity checks require complete expected folds, finite
metrics, valid probability ranges for classification, and zero paper overlap in
grouped protocols. The final refit stores its feature manifest, preprocessing
objects, candidate identity, selection provenance, and data fingerprint beside
the serialized estimator. Consequently, a reported number can be traced back to
an immutable prediction ledger, while an inference artifact can be rejected if
its schema or database fingerprint does not match.

The figure source is similarly reproducible.
Figures~\ref{fig:method-jose-mapping}--\ref{fig:method-fsr-pipeline} are
generated by \path{Report/figures/generate_method_charts.py} as vector PDFs
with embedded fonts; high-resolution PNG files are retained only as review
fallbacks. This avoids resolution loss and ensures consistent typography,
colour, line weight, and physical dimensions in the compiled report.

\section{Explainability and physical interpretation}
\label{sec:method-explainability}

Explainability is performed after evaluation; it does not influence model
selection. Tree impurity importance summarizes how often and how effectively a
feature splits training data. Permutation importance measures the loss of
held-out performance after one original feature is shuffled. SHAP decomposes
each prediction into additive feature contributions relative to a background:
\[
  f(\mathbf{x}_i)=\phi_0+\sum_j\phi_{ij}, \qquad
  I_j^{\mathrm{SHAP}}=\frac{1}{n}\sum_i|\phi_{ij}|.
\]
Beeswarm plots retain both sign and feature value, whereas mean absolute SHAP
bars show magnitude only.

These are explanations of fitted functions, not causal experiments. Correlated
raw and derived features can substitute for one another; missingness can encode
paper identity; and a physically plausible ranking does not prove a governing
mechanism. Importance is therefore reported with the model, task, and evaluation
regime that produced it rather than as a universal material ranking. The report
compares SHAP with held-out permutation importance and interprets concordant
patterns as hypotheses for testing and data collection, not as certification
evidence. No feature is described as a causal driver solely because it receives
a large attribution.

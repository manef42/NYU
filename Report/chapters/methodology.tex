\section{Assembly of the microgravity flammability database}
\label{sec:database-assembly}

\subsection{Literature screening and inclusion}

The database was assembled before either machine-learning pipeline was designed.
Approximately 130 experimental papers and technical reports published from 1966
onward were screened for solid-fuel combustion under microgravity,
partial-gravity, or spacecraft-relevant atmospheric and flow conditions. Only
about 87 publications exposed sufficiently resolved observations to map ignition
or flame-spread rate (FSR) into individual database rows. A paper was useful for
modeling only when the experiment, specimen, environment, and measured outcome
could be connected at row level; a qualitative conclusion without recoverable
conditions could support the literature review but not supervised learning.

The resulting corpus is deliberately broader than one material family or rig.
It includes wires, flat specimens, cylinders, and spheres tested on the ground,
in drop towers, parabolic aircraft, and spacecraft. Each row retains a full
human-readable citation, authors, and DOI or report identifier where available.
That provenance is essential because observations from one publication are
clustered and because ambiguous values must be checked against the source rather
than treated as anonymous numbers.

\subsection{Extraction strategy}

The first attempted extraction workflow was a conventional optical character
recognition (OCR) pipeline. It was not viable for this corpus. The sources range
from old scanned papers to long NASA memoranda; notation, units, table
structures, and page layouts differ; scans and plotted markers can be blurred;
and many results appear only in graphs rather than machine-readable text.
Generic OCR also confuses subscripts and symbols, separates values from their
headers, and cannot reliably infer which experimental condition belongs to a
plotted point. Applying it at scale would have created a large table with an
unacceptably hidden transcription error rate.

The adopted workflow was therefore AI-assisted but human-verified:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item the source was read to identify the experiment, sample, controlled
  conditions, reported outputs, and units;
  \item an AI extraction pass was used to recover as much structured information
  as possible for each experimental row;
  \item every extracted row was then checked against the paper by the database
  curator, and missing or incorrectly associated values were completed or
  corrected manually;
  \item units and categorical names were standardized while the original
  citation and explanatory notes were retained for traceability.
\end{enumerate}

AI was used as an extraction accelerator, not as an authority. This distinction
is important for scientific data: a fluent transcription can still attach a
correct number to the wrong series, silently flatten a logarithmic axis, or
invent precision that the graph does not contain. Manual review remained the
acceptance step.

For dense plots, the points were digitized with WebPlotDigitizer
\cite{webplotdigitizer}. Axis calibration was set from the published ticks,
including explicit log-axis selection where required; each color or marker
series was traced separately; overlapping points were inspected at high zoom;
and the exported coordinates were checked against axis limits and values quoted
in the text. This procedure was preferred to AI-internal OCR because exact point
coordinates, logarithmic scaling, and overlapping markers were otherwise
unreliable.

\subsection{Row structure, outputs, and quality control}

The final schema groups fields into citation, specimen, flow, apparatus,
outputs, and notes. Inputs cover geometry, dimensions, fuel and core
thermophysical properties, oxygen concentration, diluent, gas properties,
pressure, flow, dimensionless transport groups, gravity, facility, and ignition
conditions. Flammability is documented through the five outputs introduced in
\cref{tab:flammability-variables}: ignition, flame length, FSR, heat-release
rate, and smoke/aerosols. Not every paper reports every output.

The raw table uses a category banner as its first row and field names as its
second. Values were standardized to SI units wherever the source supported a
defensible conversion: length in metres, pressure in pascals, velocity in
\si{\metre\per\second}, power in watts, time in seconds, density in
\si{\kilogram\per\metre\cubed}, conductivity in
\si{\watt\per\metre\per\kelvin}, heat capacity in
\si{\joule\per\kilogram\per\kelvin}, and FSR in
\si{\metre\per\second}. Values whose physical dimension did not match a target
field were not coerced merely to fill a cell.

Quality control continued in code. Encodings were detected, empty rows and
columns removed, target labels normalized, exact modeling duplicates removed,
and unparseable values recorded as missing rather than guessed. Canonical paper
identity preferred a normalized DOI and otherwise used a normalized citation.
This produces identifiers of the form
\texttt{doi::<normalized-doi>} or
\texttt{citation::<normalized-citation>}. Cosmetic citation differences
therefore cannot place one publication in two paper groups.

The two tasks use different valid subsets. After feature/target
standardization and deduplication, ignition classification contains 4,487 rows
from 87 canonical papers (3,383 ignition and 1,104 no ignition). FSR regression
contains 2,531 rows with a numeric FSR from 66 papers. The FSR papers contribute
between 1 and 193 rows, with median 22.5. Dataset fingerprints are persisted with
the splits and evaluations so that a result cannot silently refer to a changed
database.

\section{Methods common to both machine-learning pipelines}
\label{sec:common-pipeline}

\subsection{Prediction tasks and features}

Supervised learning estimates a mapping from measured inputs
\(\mathbf{x}\) to an observed target \(y\). Here the classifier estimates
\(\widehat p=P(y_{\mathrm{ign}}=1\mid\mathbf{x})\); the regressor estimates
\(\widehat v_{\mathrm{FSR}}\). Paper identifiers, authors, DOI, facility names,
free text, and post-outcome quantities were not provided as predictors.
Flame length, HRR, and smoke/aerosols were explicitly excluded from both
modeling feature sets. The appendix provides the complete feature dictionaries.

The ignition pipeline uses a fixed manifest of 32 numeric and five categorical
features. The FSR pipeline detects 29 numeric and four categorical fields from
the standardized schema. Both represent fuel and core properties, geometry,
oxygen and diluent, pressure, gas transport, flow, gravity, and ignition
conditions where available. The persisted FSR manifest also contains
categorical \texttt{Ignition}; because this conflicts with explanatory
documentation, it is disclosed as a schema limitation rather than hidden.

\subsection{Training, validation, and untouched testing}

For a reader new to machine learning, three data roles must remain distinct:
\begin{description}
  \item[Training data] fit model parameters, such as tree splits or neural
  weights.
  \item[Validation data] choose hyperparameters, such as tree depth,
  regularization, or neighborhood size.
  \item[Test data] measure the selected procedure once on observations not used
  for either fitting or selection.
\end{description}

The project implements this separation with \emph{nested} evaluation. Each
outer split creates a training partition and an untouched test partition.
Inside the outer training partition, inner folds compare hyperparameters.
Preprocessing, imputation, scaling, one-hot category vocabularies, class or
paper weights, and classification thresholds are all learned inside training
data. The selected inner configuration is then refitted on the complete outer
training partition and evaluated once on the outer test partition. Repeating
this process produces a distribution of honest outer-fold scores.

Numeric missing values are replaced by the training-fold median for models that
cannot handle missing values; XGBoost retains native numeric missingness.
Distance-, margin-, and neural-network models use training-fold standardization.
Categorical missingness becomes an explicit ``Unknown'' level, and one-hot
encoding safely ignores a category first encountered in a test fold.

\subsection{Interpolation versus extrapolation}

Both pipelines answer two different questions:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Interpolation} holds out rows while allowing other rows from
  the same paper to remain in training. It asks whether the model predicts new
  conditions inside campaigns it partly knows.
  \item \textbf{Extrapolation} holds out complete canonical papers. It asks
  whether the procedure transfers to a new publication, rig, or campaign and is
  the primary generalization test.
\end{itemize}

The distinction prevents paper-specific sampling and apparatus signatures from
being mistaken for broad physical transfer. All candidates within a task use
the same frozen outer assignments. The split files assert full row coverage,
no row overlap, and zero paper overlap in grouped protocols.

\subsection{Candidate comparison, uncertainty, and refitting}

The configured portfolios cover XGBoost, random forest or decision tree,
\(k\)-nearest neighbors (KNN), multilayer perceptron (MLP), and support-vector
models. Recipes test different loss functions, paper weights, class weights,
target transformations, and bootstrap conditions. The persisted comparison
pools are narrower than the YAML portfolios: the merged FSR evidence contains
25 candidates, and the formal ignition selection record omits available MLP
shards. Champion claims therefore apply only to candidates in the persisted
selection evidence.

Outer-fold means and standard deviations summarize repeat-to-repeat variation.
Bootstrap intervals use rows for interpolation and canonical papers for
extrapolation, preserving the relevant clustered unit. Only after model
selection is complete is the chosen configuration refitted on all labeled rows
to create an inference artifact. That full-data model is deployable, but its
training fit is not used as evidence of generalization.

\section{Ignition-classification pipeline}
\label{sec:classification-method}

Classification returns a probability rather than a physical yes/no switch.
To produce a binary decision, a threshold \(\tau\) is applied:
\[
  \widehat y(\tau)=
  \begin{cases}
  1,&\widehat p\geq\tau,\\
  0,&\widehat p<\tau.
  \end{cases}
\]
A lower threshold detects more ignitions but usually increases false alarms; a
higher threshold does the reverse. Four operating points are selected from
inner out-of-fold predictions: maximum Matthews correlation coefficient (MCC),
maximum F1, maximum balanced accuracy, and maximum Youden
\(J=\mathrm{sensitivity}+\mathrm{specificity}-1\). The outer test labels are
never used to choose \(\tau\). Threshold-independent comparison uses ROC--AUC,
PR--AUC, and Brier score; thresholded reporting adds sensitivity, specificity,
precision, F1, balanced accuracy, and MCC.

\subsection{Interpolation}

Ignition interpolation uses five-fold stratified cross-validation repeated for
seeds 42--44, giving 15 outer folds. Stratification keeps the ignition/no-
ignition proportion similar in each fold while rows are assigned independently.
Within each outer training partition, stratified inner folds search candidate
hyperparameters. Candidates are ordered by mean inner ROC--AUC, then PR--AUC,
then lower Brier score. The four thresholds are frozen from the winning inner
out-of-fold probabilities before the model is applied to the outer test fold.
A separate stratified 80/20 holdout for each seed serves as a quick consistency
check, not the primary selection protocol.

Interpolation selection requires valid probabilities, complete folds, passed
integrity checks, and ROC--AUC fold standard deviation no greater than 0.15.
Candidates within 0.01 of the best ROC--AUC are resolved by PR--AUC,
uncertainty, and a declared simplicity order. The resulting champion is then
refitted on all ignition-labeled rows for within-domain inference.

\subsection{Extrapolation}

Ignition extrapolation uses \texttt{StratifiedGroupKFold}: all rows from one
paper stay together while each fold retains both classes where feasible. Five
paper-grouped folds repeated across three seeds again produce 15 outer tests.
The inner search is also paper-disjoint, preventing a paper held out for inner
validation from appearing in the corresponding inner training fold.

The primary metric remains ROC--AUC, but eligibility additionally requires a
successful leave-one-paper-out (LOPO) robustness analysis. In LOPO, each of the
87 papers is held out once. A single-class paper cannot define its own ROC--AUC,
so LOPO is interpreted through pooled predictions, calibration, and the subset
of papers for which a metric is defined. The selected extrapolation model and
its thresholds are refitted on the full labeled cohort only after this
paper-disjoint comparison is frozen.

\section{Flame-spread-rate regression pipeline}
\label{sec:regression-method}

Regression predicts a continuous FSR in \si{\metre\per\second}. There is no
classification threshold: prediction quality is measured directly in target
units. RMSE penalizes larger errors more strongly, MAE reports typical absolute
error, \(R^2\) compares residual variance with a mean predictor, and mean bias
error records systematic over- or underprediction. MAPE is retained only as a
secondary diagnostic because exact and near-zero FSR values make percentage
errors unstable.

Every persisted FSR candidate is evaluated on real training data
(\texttt{rd}) and on \texttt{rd\_bt}, which appends 1,000 training rows sampled
with replacement. Copies inherit their source paper and never enter the test
partition. Bootstrap augmentation therefore changes empirical weighting; it
does not create a new experiment or extend physical support.

\subsection{Interpolation}

FSR interpolation uses ten repeated, approximately 80/20 row holdouts with
seeds 42--51. Target values are binned into six quantiles where feasible so that
each split represents the strongly skewed FSR distribution. Papers may occur
on both sides. Inside each outer training partition, shuffled \(K\)-fold
validation compares hyperparameters by lower RMSE, then lower MAE, then higher
\(R^2\). The selected recipe is fitted on all 2,024 training rows and tested on
the 507 untouched rows for that repeat.

The selection policy rejects incomplete or non-finite evaluations, RMSE
coefficient of variation above 0.45, and mean \(R^2<-0.25\). Candidates within
2\% of the lowest RMSE form a tie band resolved by higher \(R^2\), lower
uncertainty, preference for real data, simpler family, and fewer features where
the feature sets differ.

\subsection{Extrapolation}

FSR extrapolation also uses ten repeated 80/20 holdouts, but the unit assigned
to train or test is the paper. For each repeat, 50 candidate
\texttt{GroupShuffleSplit} partitions are generated and the most target-balanced
paper-disjoint split is retained. Each test contains 14 papers and each training
partition 52; row counts vary with paper size. Balance screening reduces
avoidable target-range changes without relaxing paper separation.

Inner tuning uses \texttt{GroupKFold} by paper. The extrapolation tie-breaks add
the interpolation--extrapolation gap so that a recipe is not favored solely for
excellent within-paper fit. Cluster bootstrap intervals resample papers rather
than thousands of correlated rows. Finally, the winning extrapolation
configuration is refitted on all 2,531 FSR rows and stored separately from the
outer-fold evidence.

\section{Explainability and physical interpretation}
\label{sec:method-explainability}

Explainability is performed after evaluation; it does not influence model
selection. Tree impurity importance summarizes how often and how effectively a
feature splits training data. Permutation importance measures the loss of
held-out performance after one original feature is shuffled. SHAP decomposes
each prediction into additive feature contributions relative to a background:
\[
  f(\mathbf{x}_i)=\phi_0+\sum_j\phi_{ij}, \qquad
  I_j^{\mathrm{SHAP}}=\frac{1}{n}\sum_i|\phi_{ij}|.
\]
Beeswarm plots retain both sign and feature value, whereas mean absolute SHAP
bars show magnitude only.

These are explanations of fitted functions, not causal experiments. Correlated
raw and derived features can substitute for one another; missingness can encode
paper identity; and a physically plausible ranking does not prove a governing
mechanism. The report therefore compares SHAP with held-out permutation
importance and interprets repeated patterns as hypotheses for testing and data
collection, not as certification evidence.

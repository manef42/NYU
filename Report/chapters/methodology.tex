\chapter{Methodology}
\label{chap:methodology}

\section{Study design and prediction tasks}
\label{sec:method-study-design}

This work treats the curated microgravity-combustion database as an observational,
multi-study data set rather than as a collection of independent and identically
distributed measurements.  An experimental paper commonly contributes a series of
closely related rows obtained with the same apparatus, material preparation,
diagnostic practice, and range of operating conditions.  Consequently, two rows
from one paper can be substantially more similar than two rows drawn from different
papers even when their tabulated physical variables overlap.  The methodology was
therefore organized around two distinct prediction tasks.  The first was binary
classification of whether ignition occurred.  The second was regression of flame
spread rate (FSR), expressed in \(\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}\).  FSR was modeled only where
a numeric FSR observation was present.  No extinction target or extinction model
was defined.

For each task, interpolation and extrapolation were evaluated separately.
\emph{Row interpolation} asks how accurately a model predicts a held-out row when
other rows from the same paper may remain in training.  It is useful for estimating
performance within the support of campaigns already represented in the database,
but it does not test transfer to a new publication.  \emph{Paper-grouped
extrapolation} instead holds out complete canonical papers, so that no paper
identity occurs in both training and test partitions.  It asks whether relationships
learned from the available literature transfer to a previously unseen paper or
campaign.  The grouped estimate is the primary evidence for cross-paper
generalization.  The deliberate use of both protocols makes the otherwise hidden
effect of campaign dependence measurable.

The two pipelines followed the same high-level sequence: load and standardize the
data; freeze all outer partitions; perform hyperparameter search within each outer
training partition; fit the selected configuration to that partition; evaluate it
once on the untouched outer test partition; aggregate persisted predictions and
metrics; choose separate interpolation and extrapolation champions by a declared
policy; and only then refit deployable models on all labeled rows.  Evaluation
models and full-data refits have different inferential roles.  The former provide
out-of-sample comparison evidence, while the latter are operational artifacts and
do not supply unbiased performance estimates.

\section{Database ingestion and canonicalization}
\label{sec:method-data}

\subsection{Source structure and evidenced standardization}

Both analyses used the task-local \texttt{Microgravity\_Database.csv}.  The CSV
contains a category banner in its first row, a field-name header in its second row,
and observations thereafter.  The loaders therefore read the CSV after skipping
the category row and attempted UTF-8, UTF-8 with byte-order mark, CP1252, and
Latin-1 encodings.  The persisted validation reports identify CP1252 as the
successful encoding for both analyzed files.  The FSR loader additionally accepts
Excel workbooks with one- or two-level headers, but the persisted analysis reported
the CSV parse \texttt{skiprows=1; encoding=cp1252}.  These operations describe
schema ingestion and computational standardization; the repository does not
document enough of the upstream literature-search or manual transcription process
to justify broader claims about database construction.

Whitespace and placeholder tokens were normalized, duplicate headers and all-empty
columns were removed where applicable, and numeric values were parsed from
unit-bearing cells.  The ignition pipeline implemented explicit conversion for
sample dimensions to metres, pressure to pascals, and flow velocity to
\(\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}\), using either a recognized cell suffix or the unit in the
column header.  Gravity was represented relative to terrestrial gravity,
\(g/g_{\mathrm{Earth}}\).  A microgravity text token without a numeric value was
mapped to \(10^{-6}\), while acceleration values in \(\mathrm{m\,s^{-2}}\),
\(\mathrm{cm\,s^{-2}}\), or \(\mathrm{mm\,s^{-2}}\) were converted to this relative
scale.  Ignition-power entries describing heat flux, current, or other quantities
that were not watts were not coerced into power values.  In the FSR pipeline,
columns were classified as numeric when at least 60\% of their available cells
could be parsed from a leading number.  Restricting parsing to a leading numeric
token prevents chemical labels such as \(\mathrm{N_2}\) or \(\mathrm{CO_2}\) from
being mistaken for measurements.  Thus, for example, ``101.3 kPa'' is numeric
while ``N2'' remains categorical.

Six fields were dropped before modeling in both tasks: material of sample, rig
name, internal geometry, internal dimensions, facility, and free-form information.
Paper identifiers and bibliographic fingerprints were never supplied as predictors.
For ignition, the post-outcome columns flame length, FSR, heat-release rate (HRR),
and smoke/aerosols were explicitly excluded.  For FSR, the selected FSR target,
duplicate FSR-like fields, citation and author identifiers, notes, and detected
post-outcome fields such as flame length, HRR, smoke/aerosols, and extinction-like
fields were excluded.  This exclusion is a leakage control, not an extinction
analysis.

\subsection{Canonical paper identity}

The grouping unit was a canonical paper identifier.  A valid DOI was normalized by
removing URL or \texttt{doi:} prefixes, lowercasing, and retaining the canonical
DOI expression.  The resulting identifier had the form
\texttt{doi::<normalized-doi>}.  If no valid DOI was available, the citation was
lowercased, punctuation was removed, whitespace was collapsed, and the identifier
\texttt{citation::<normalized-citation>} was used.  Raw DOI strings were therefore
not used directly as groups.  This canonicalization is essential: cosmetic
differences in citation formatting must not permit one publication to appear under
multiple group labels and thereby cross a grouped train--test boundary.

\subsection{Task-specific cohorts}

The ignition target was the database field \texttt{Ignition}, mapped to
\(y_i\in\{0,1\}\) for No and Yes.  Training required every target to be recognized.
Rows were deduplicated on the complete engineered feature vector, target, and
canonical paper identifier, retaining the first occurrence.  The persisted
\texttt{fable-data-v5} cohort contains 4,487 observations from 87 papers after 570
duplicates were removed.  There were 3,383 ignition-positive and 1,104
ignition-negative rows, corresponding to a positive prevalence of 0.75396.
Twenty-nine blank source rows were also identified in the validation report.

For regression, the loader selected the FSR-like column with the greatest number of
numeric values; the persisted target was \texttt{FSR (m/s)}.  Rows lacking numeric
FSR were removed, and duplicates were counted after target filtering.  The
\texttt{fsr-data-v2} cohort contains 2,531 usable rows from 66 papers, derived from
5,057 raw rows: 2,467 rows lacked FSR and 59 duplicates were removed.  Papers
contributed between 1 and 193 usable rows (median 22.5, mean 38.35), demonstrating
substantial imbalance in campaign size.  The observed FSR ranged from 0 to
\(0.211~\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}\), with mean \(0.008774~\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}\) and
standard deviation \(0.017252~\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}\).

\section{Predictor representation and preprocessing}
\label{sec:method-features}

\subsection{Ignition feature engineering}

Ignition classification used a fixed manifest of 32 numeric and five categorical
features.  This manifest was invariant across all candidates.  Fuel properties
comprised density (\(\mathrm{kg\,m^{-3}}\)), thermal conductivity
(\(\mathrm{W\,m^{-1}\,K^{-1}}\)), specific heat
(\(\mathrm{J\,kg^{-1}\,K^{-1}}\)), pyrolysis temperature (K), thermal diffusivity
(\(\mathrm{m^2\,s^{-1}}\)), and volumetric heat capacity
(\(\mathrm{J\,m^{-3}\,K^{-1}}\)).  Corresponding core properties included density,
thermal conductivity, specific heat, and volumetric heat capacity.

Gas and transport variables were oxygen concentration, gas molar mass, gas
specific heat, gas thermal conductivity, gas density, gas thermal diffusivity,
kinematic viscosity, Reynolds number, Péclet number, Prandtl number, and thermal
diffusion time in seconds.  Operating conditions comprised pressure in pascals,
signed flow velocity and absolute flow speed in \(\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}\), relative
gravity, ignition power in watts, and ignition duration in seconds.  Geometry was
represented by half-thickness, a characteristic length, and as many as three
parsed sample dimensions, all in metres.

The five categorical variables were engineered from the schema: sample geometry
(wire, flat, cylindrical, spherical, or residual category), diluent, signed-flow
direction (coflow, counterflow, or quiescent), gravity regime (microgravity,
partial gravity, Earth gravity, or hypergravity/unknown), and ignition method
(open flame, radiative heater, discharge, wire/coil, or unknown).  Signed flow and
absolute flow were intentionally both retained because direction and magnitude
encode different information.  Derived quantities already present in the database,
including Reynolds, Péclet, and Prandtl numbers and thermal diffusion time, were
treated as predictors; their inclusion creates correlated representations and must
be considered when interpreting importance.

Missingness was heterogeneous.  Flow variables were missing in 897 ignition rows,
gravity in 54, and gas-derived transport variables in 48.  Ignition power was
missing in 3,606 rows and ignition time in 2,400; the third sample dimension was
missing in 2,049.  By contrast, many tabulated material properties had no recorded
missing values.  These patterns mean that apparatus-related variables can partly
encode which campaigns measured or reported them, even though explicit paper
identifiers were excluded.

\subsection{FSR feature manifest}

FSR regression used a detected manifest rather than the ignition pipeline's renamed
fixed manifest.  The persisted manifest contains 29 numeric and four categorical
fields.  Numeric fields cover the sample-dimension string parsed numerically,
half-thickness and characteristic length; fuel and core thermophysical properties;
oxygen concentration; gas molar mass, heat capacity, conductivity, density,
thermal diffusivity, and kinematic viscosity; Reynolds, Péclet, and Prandtl
numbers; thermal diffusion time; pressure in pascals; flow velocity in
\(\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}\); relative gravity; and ignition power and time.  Geometry,
diluent, ignition method, and ignition outcome are categorical.  The manifest tags
ignition power, ignition time, ignition method, and ignition outcome as apparatus
features and the remainder as physics features.  However, role tags were retained
for reporting only: all candidates used the same full feature list, so the
\texttt{feature\_set=all} designation is substantive and no physics-only feature
ablation was performed.

The FSR target is in \(\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}\), and names carrying SI units were
interpreted accordingly.  The generic FSR parser extracts leading numbers but does
not establish additional unit conversions beyond the recorded schema; no stronger
standardization claim is made.  Missingness again concentrated in apparatus and
flow fields: among analyzed rows, flow was missing in 709, ignition power in 2,198,
ignition time in 1,725, gravity in 30, and several gas/transport variables in 33.

\subsection{Fold-local transformations}

All learned preprocessing was fitted on training data only.  For scikit-learn-style
models, numeric missing values were replaced by the training-fold median.
XGBoost retained native missing values.  Numeric scaling by a training-fold
\texttt{StandardScaler} was applied for distance-, margin-, and neural-network
families, but not for tree models.  Categorical missingness was replaced by the
constant ``Unknown'' and categories were one-hot encoded with unknown-category-safe
handling.  This arrangement prevents test-set medians or category vocabularies
from entering model fitting.

\section{Candidate models, weighting, and augmentation}
\label{sec:method-models}

The ignition configuration declared 26 candidates spanning XGBoost,
decision trees, \(k\)-nearest neighbors (KNN), multilayer perceptrons (MLP), and
support-vector machines (SVM).  Candidate recipes varied paper weighting, balanced
class weighting, focal versus logistic XGBoost objectives, distance-weighted KNN,
and linear versus radial-basis SVM.  The FSR configuration declared 34 candidates:
10 XGBoost, seven random-forest, six KNN, five MLP, and six support-vector
regression (SVR) recipes.  Regression candidates varied paper weighting, tree loss,
signed-\(\log(1+y)\) target transformation, KNN distance weighting, SVR kernel, and
XGBoost paper bagging.  Predictions from a transformed-target candidate were
inverse-transformed to original FSR units before evaluation.

The persisted comparison pools were smaller than these configured portfolios.
The merged FSR manifest contains 25 candidates (50 candidate--augmentation
conditions), and the formal ignition selection record omits MLP candidates even
though MLP shard metrics are present.  Results and champion claims in this report
therefore refer to the candidates actually represented in the persisted
leaderboards and selection records, not to an assumed complete execution of every
configured recipe.

Campaign-size weighting was defined from the number \(n_p\) of training rows in
paper \(p\).  The principal choices were
\begin{equation}
  w_{ip}^{(\mathrm{none})}=1,\qquad
  w_{ip}^{(\mathrm{sqrt})}=n_p^{-1/2},\qquad
  w_{ip}^{(\mathrm{inverse})}=n_p^{-1}.
  \label{eq:paper-weights}
\end{equation}
For ignition, optional balanced class weights were multiplied by the paper weights
and the result renormalized.  Families with native sample-weight support received
these values during fitting.  Weight-blind families used deterministic
probability-proportional resampling so that a weighting option was not silently
ignored.  The FSR MLP minimized weighted mean squared error; FSR KNN and SVR used
the analogous resampling path.

Every FSR candidate was evaluated under two training-data conditions.
\texttt{rd} used the real outer-training rows.  \texttt{rd\_bt} appended 1,000
rows sampled with replacement from those training rows; each copy retained its
source paper identifier.  This bootstrap operation changes the empirical
frequency of observed conditions.  It neither generates independent experiments
nor expands physical support, and the test set was never augmented.  Candidate and
augmentation together formed the regression selection unit.

\section{Frozen outer evaluation protocols}
\label{sec:method-splits}

The ignition split file used base seed 42 and three consecutive seeds.  For each
seed, it stored a stratified 80/20 row holdout, a five-fold stratified row
cross-validation, and a five-fold stratified grouped cross-validation.  It also
stored 87 leave-one-paper-out (LOPO) folds, one for each canonical paper.  Thus,
the primary interpolation and grouped protocols each comprised 15 outer folds,
the supplementary holdout comprised three, and LOPO supplied a paper-level
robustness analysis.  Split integrity was checked for all 120 split objects.

The FSR pipeline generated ten repeated 80/20 holdouts using seeds 42--51.  The
interpolation partition was stratified by six target quantile bins when feasible
and consistently contained 2,024 training and 507 test rows.  For extrapolation,
50 candidate \texttt{GroupShuffleSplit} partitions were generated per repeat and
the most target-balanced paper-disjoint candidate was retained.  Each grouped
training set contained 52 papers and each test set 14 papers, although row counts
varied because paper sizes differed.  Balance screening reduces avoidable changes
in target distribution without weakening the defining paper-disjoint constraint.
Integrity checks across all 20 partitions confirmed full coverage, nonempty
partitions, no row overlap, and, for grouped partitions, no paper overlap.

\section{Nested tuning and leakage control}
\label{sec:method-nested}

Hyperparameters were selected separately inside every outer training partition.
For ignition interpolation, inner stratified folds were used; grouped
extrapolation and LOPO used stratified, paper-disjoint inner folds with both classes
represented.  For FSR, shuffled K-fold inner validation was used for interpolation
and \texttt{GroupKFold} by paper for extrapolation.  Up to 40 distinct parameter
configurations were sampled from each family search space in the documented full
workflow.  Ignition used three inner grouped folds; FSR used five inner folds.

For ignition configuration \(h\), the inner score ordered candidates by mean
receiver-operating-characteristic area under the curve (ROC--AUC), then
precision--recall AUC (PR--AUC), then lower Brier score.  For FSR it ordered by
lower root mean squared error (RMSE), then lower mean absolute error (MAE), then
higher coefficient of determination \(R^2\).  The best configuration was refitted
on the complete outer training partition and evaluated once on the outer test
partition.  Search histories, selected hyperparameters, outer predictions, and
integrity status were persisted.  Dataset SHA-256 fingerprints linked the input,
splits, and evaluations; the FSR evaluator explicitly aborted on a mismatch.

For the ignition task, decision thresholds were also selected without outer-test
labels.  Given a probability estimate \(\hat p_i\),
\begin{equation}
  \hat y_i(\tau)=\mathbb{I}\!\left(\hat p_i\geq\tau\right).
  \label{eq:threshold}
\end{equation}
Four thresholds maximized, respectively, Matthews correlation coefficient (MCC),
F1 score, balanced accuracy, and Youden's
\(J=\mathrm{sensitivity}+\mathrm{specificity}-1\) on the winning configuration's
inner out-of-fold predictions.  Ties favored a threshold nearer 0.5.  Once frozen,
each threshold was applied to the outer test fold.  No single threshold was
declared universally optimal because each policy imposes a different balance
between false positives and false negatives.

\section{Metrics, uncertainty, and model selection}
\label{sec:method-metrics}

Threshold-independent classification metrics were ROC--AUC, PR--AUC, and the
Brier score
\begin{equation}
  \mathrm{Brier}=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}(\hat p_i-y_i)^2.
  \label{eq:brier}
\end{equation}
Thresholded metrics included MCC, F1, balanced accuracy, sensitivity, specificity,
and precision.  Regression metrics included
\begin{align}
  \mathrm{RMSE} &=
    \sqrt{\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}(y_i-\hat y_i)^2}, &
  \mathrm{MAE} &=
    \frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}|y_i-\hat y_i|, \label{eq:reg-errors}\\
  R^2 &=1-\frac{\sum_i(y_i-\hat y_i)^2}
                    {\sum_i(y_i-\bar y)^2}, &
  \mathrm{MBE} &=\frac{1}{n}\sum_i(\hat y_i-y_i).
  \label{eq:r2-mbe}
\end{align}
MAPE and normalized RMSE were also persisted.  MAPE is unstable when measured FSR
approaches zero and was therefore descriptive rather than a champion-selection
criterion.

Reported ``mean \(\pm\) SD'' values summarize outer-fold variability.  In
addition, 2,000-resample 95\% bootstrap intervals were computed from persisted
outer predictions.  Repeated predictions for a row were averaged before
resampling.  Interpolation resampled unique rows; extrapolation resampled canonical
papers, preserving the clustered inferential unit.  Fold SD and cluster-bootstrap
intervals answer different questions and are both reported where available.

Ignition candidates had to pass integrity checks, complete all required folds,
produce valid probabilities, and have ROC--AUC fold SD no greater than 0.15.
Interpolation was ranked on stratified-row ROC--AUC.  Extrapolation was ranked on
grouped ROC--AUC and additionally required successful LOPO evaluation.  Values
within 0.01 of the best were resolved by higher PR--AUC, lower uncertainty, and a
declared simplicity order.  FSR candidates required complete, finite evaluation,
RMSE coefficient of variation no greater than 0.45, and mean \(R^2\geq-0.25\).
Any RMSE within 2\% of the best formed a tie band.  Tie-breakers considered higher
\(R^2\), lower uncertainty, and no augmentation; extrapolation additionally
considered a smaller interpolation--extrapolation gap.  Because all persisted FSR
candidates used the same full feature set, the nominal physics-only and
fewer-features tie-breakers did not distinguish them.  For both tasks, refitting
used the modal outer-fold hyperparameter configuration, with lexical resolution
of an exact modal tie.

\section{Post hoc explainability}
\label{sec:method-explainability}

Tree-model explanations were generated from persisted evaluation and refit
artifacts, without retuning.  Mean absolute SHAP magnitude was used as a global
summary,
\begin{equation}
  I_j^{\mathrm{SHAP}}=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}|\phi_{ij}|,
  \label{eq:shap}
\end{equation}
where \(\phi_{ij}\) is the additive contribution assigned to feature \(j\) for
observation \(i\).  Permutation importance measured the degradation in held-out
score after disrupting one original feature while leaving the fitted model fixed.
For classification, positive values are losses in ROC--AUC; for regression,
positive values are increases in RMSE.  Repeated permutations provide the reported
mean and standard deviation.

These measures are predictive, conditional on the fitted model and sampled data.
They do not identify causal effects.  Correlated physical and engineered variables
can divide, substitute, or mask importance; one-hot levels and their parent
categorical fields have different granularities; missingness can carry campaign
information; and SHAP magnitude does not establish the sign of an effect.
Negative permutation importance means that random disruption happened to improve
held-out performance under the realized sample, not that the variable is
physically protective.  Explainability was consequently interpreted as evidence
about model reliance and predictive associations only.

\section{Reproducibility, estimands, and reporting conventions}
\label{sec:method-reproducibility}

Reproducibility was enforced at the level of data identity, partition membership,
candidate definition, and randomization.  The default random seed was 42; repeated
splits used consecutive integer seeds.  Split assignments stored the row and paper
membership of every partition rather than relying on regeneration from an
algorithm description.  Candidate manifests recorded model family, weighting,
fixed parameters, and augmentation condition.  Search histories and selected
hyperparameters preserved the choices made independently for each outer fold.
Integrity files recorded failed fits or invalid outputs instead of replacing them
with fabricated scores.  This persisted-state design permits the report to be
regenerated from evaluation outputs and fitted artifacts without rerunning or
altering hyperparameter optimization.

The relevant estimand differs by protocol.  In a row split, observations are the
resampling units and papers can be represented in both partitions.  Its performance
is therefore conditional on the mixture of campaigns already present.  In a
grouped split, the held-out units are papers; rows within a paper remain dependent
and should not be counted as independent evidence for cross-paper uncertainty.
The use of canonical-paper bootstrap resampling for grouped predictions reflects
this distinction.  It does not eliminate all dependence---for example, multiple
papers may share investigators, rigs, materials, or facilities---but it avoids the
more serious error of assigning independent status to repeated rows from one
publication.

Fold means, fold standard deviations, and pooled-prediction bootstrap estimates
were kept conceptually separate.  If \(m_k\) is a metric in outer fold \(k\), the
reported fold summary is
\begin{equation}
  \bar m=\frac{1}{K}\sum_{k=1}^{K}m_k,\qquad
  s_m=\sqrt{\frac{1}{K-1}\sum_{k=1}^{K}(m_k-\bar m)^2}.
  \label{eq:fold-summary}
\end{equation}
This gives each repeat or fold one contribution.  The bootstrap tables instead
average repeated predictions for a row, recompute a metric on the pooled
predictions, and resample the appropriate row or paper units.  Because nonlinear
metrics generally do not commute with averaging, a bootstrap point estimate need
not reproduce \(\bar m\).  The former describes variability among the frozen
outer evaluations; the latter supplies a sampling interval under the stated
cluster-resampling scheme.

All rates and errors were reported in the units of their persisted targets.
Ignition discrimination metrics are dimensionless, and the Brier score is a mean
squared probability error.  FSR RMSE, MAE, and MBE retain
\(\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}\); \(R^2\), MAPE, and NRMSE are dimensionless.  Candidate
names such as \texttt{baseline}, \texttt{sqrt}, and \texttt{inverse} identify
computational recipes rather than physical hypotheses.  Similarly,
\texttt{rd\_bt} denotes duplicated training observations sampled with replacement,
not a second experimental cohort.  These conventions prevent changes in
evaluation protocol, aggregation, or units from being mistaken for changes in the
underlying physical phenomenon.
